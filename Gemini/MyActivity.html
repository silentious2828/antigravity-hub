A typhoon is a mature tropical cyclone that develops in the northwestern Pacific Ocean with sustained winds of at least 74 mph (119 km/h). It is the exact same weather phenomenon as a hurricane, but the name changes based on where the storm forms in the world.

How Typhoons Form

  • Warm water: Sea surface temperatures must reach at least 80°F (27°C).
  • Rising air: Warm, moist air over the ocean rises and creates a low-pressure zone.
  • Rotation: The Earth's rotation spins the incoming air into a powerful circular system.

Structure of a Typhoon

  • The Eye: A calm, clear center with low pressure.
  • The Eyewall: Ring of towering thunderstorms surrounding the eye with the strongest winds and heaviest rain.
  • Spiral Rainbands: Outer bands of clouds stretching out and producing heavy downpours and gusts.

Regional Names for Tropical Cyclones

  • Typhoon: Northwest Pacific Ocean (East and Southeast Asia).
  • Hurricane: North Atlantic and Northeast/Central Pacific Ocean.
  • Cyclone: Indian Ocean and South Pacific Ocean.
